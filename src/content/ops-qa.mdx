## Where it fits in Soundboard

This is the other half of client operations, alongside the client summary pipeline. It reads
the current conversation thread and the firm's own precedent corpus, and writes a drafted
client reply for an admin to review, edit, or discard before it ever reaches a client.

## Problem and origin

Admins were drafting every client reply from scratch, in a regulated domain where a wrong
answer has real financial and legal consequences. I built a retrieval-augmented system that
drafts high-quality, on-brand client communications instantly, grounded in the firm's own
historical precedent, with a full audit trail.

## Constraints

Every draft has to be grounded in real precedent, never invented or hallucinated as fact, and
never contaminated by the system's own prior output. Redaction has to happen before any client
message becomes shared precedent, so another client's data can never leak across accounts. A
human has to stay the last word on every message before a client sees it.

## Architecture

The platform is a dual-service, purpose-separated architecture: a TypeScript service
(Express, Prisma, PostgreSQL) owns orchestration, durable state, the database of record, and
feedback capture; a Python service (FastAPI) owns all AI operations, embeddings, retrieval, and
generation.

The pipeline runs as one fused server call: read the full thread context, construct a
style-aware query (client voice versus admin voice, fresh reply versus proactive follow-up),
run dense retrieval across the firm's precedent corpus, score confidence as strong, moderate,
or weak, generate a grounded draft from real precedent and the real thread, and hand it to the
admin to accept, edit, or discard. Every outcome is captured automatically and the sent message
becomes tomorrow's precedent.

Retrieval is multi-tier: a single query runs concurrently across client-scoped,
entity-type-scoped, tax-year-scoped, and fully unscoped searches, ranked by a fused score
rather than tier order alone, since a strong match at a looser tier can outrank a weak match at
a stricter one. Every tier enforces a hard similarity floor. If nothing clears it, the system
reports "no strong precedent found" rather than forcing a low-confidence match. The engine also
distinguishes terse, instruction-style admin queries from natural, conversational client
queries and routes each to the vector space that matches its linguistic register, and it
recognizes when an admin wants a proactive follow-up with no fresh client question to reply to,
rerouting the whole retrieval and generation strategy accordingly.

On generation, the model always drafts, even with zero precedent, surfacing weak grounding
through the confidence tier rather than refusing outright. Every specific fact, figure, date,
or account detail must come from the current conversation or the admin's own notes, precedent
text is guidance for tone and structure only, and the model is explicitly instructed to
recognize and never reproduce another client's redacted placeholder tokens.

On privacy, legal names, first names, last names, trailing initials, and business names are
automatically detected and redacted before any message becomes shared precedent. The system is
also architecturally incapable of citing its own prior AI-generated output as precedent: any
precedent traced back to an accepted or edited AI suggestion is automatically excluded from
future retrieval, a guard against silent quality drift.

## Key decisions and tradeoffs

I deliberately engineered the improvement loop around provider-independent, human-governed
calibration rather than any single vendor's fine-tuning offering. Every suggestion is logged
with its full retrieval evidence, every admin action (accept, edit, or let it lapse) is
captured automatically, and an edit-distance measurement turns "was this any good?" into an
objective signal that a calibration reporting engine aggregates against live confidence
thresholds. A human expert reviews the report and tunes the live configuration, a fast,
reversible adjustment with no dependency on third-party retraining infrastructure. The
tradeoff is that improvement is gated on a human review step rather than fully automatic, which
is the point: no unsupervised self-learning from the system's own output.

## Evaluation and reliability

Every retrieval and generation call is wrapped in structured observability spans capturing
similarity thresholds, tier counts, token usage, latency, and cost. Every suggestion request
carries an idempotency key, so retries from a flaky connection or a double-click are safe with
no duplicate generation cost. The default posture is zero-retry, fast-timeout, so a single
request can't silently multiply LLM spend, and suggestions an admin never acts on are
automatically expired on a rolling schedule. Every payload between the two services is
schema-validated end to end.

## Results

Live in production, actively drafting real client communications. The feedback-capture and
calibration-reporting infrastructure is fully deployed and already recording real admin
interactions (accept/edit outcomes, quantified edit distances, and precedent lineage), building
the dataset the calibration loop is designed to act on. No accept-rate or edit-distance outcome
metric yet; that lands once enough real usage data accumulates.

## What I'd do differently

Accumulate enough real-world feedback volume to drive the first data-backed threshold
calibration, extend structured feedback capture to the reject pathway for a complete quality
picture, and expand golden-set backtesting coverage as the precedent corpus grows.
